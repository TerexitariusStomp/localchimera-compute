2. Your identity, and the passcode that is its only key

Creating an identity mints a KERI autonomic identifier (an "AID"), which is what names you everywhere in the system. Your browser generates a random passcode and derives your signing keys from it locally. The passcode is never transmitted to us and never stored on any server. We hold your public keys; we never hold your private ones.

There is no password reset, and there is no recovery. Nobody — not the operator, not the authors — can restore access to an identity whose passcode has been lost, because nobody else ever had it. If you lose the passcode, the identity, the credentials attached to it, and any labour credit recorded against it are unreachable, permanently. Save the recovery file or put the AID and passcode in a password manager when they are shown to you. They are shown once.

You are responsible for:

  • keeping the passcode secret and durable — treat it as you would a house key you cannot re-cut;
  • everything done by your identity, since a valid signature from your key is, to every other participant, indistinguishable from you;
  • telling us promptly if you believe your key has been compromised, so we can help you rotate it (KERI supports key rotation; it is the intended response to compromise, and it works only while you still control the current key).

3. Verification and vouching

To keep out spam and vote-stuffing, a member's standing as a unique real person is attested by other verified members. If you vouch for someone, or grant them verification, you are making a signed statement that you have grounds to believe they are a distinct real person. Do not vouch for identities you cannot vouch for. Vouching falsely — to inflate voting weight, to claim goods twice, or to admit someone excluded by governance — is a breach of these terms and is visible in the record.

"Verified" means that other members said so. It is a peer's statement about uniqueness. It is not an identity check by the operator, a background check, a certificate of character or competence, or a guarantee about anyone's conduct, and nobody should treat it as one (§9.2).

4. What becomes public, and what cannot be taken back

This is the section most unlike an ordinary service, and the one worth reading twice.

4.1 Your key history is published

Every AID has a key event log (KEL): its creation, and every subsequent key rotation, each signed and timestamped. The KEL is served over this gateway's discovery endpoint and is broadcast to witnesses — independent machines, run by other people, potentially in other countries — whose whole purpose is to hold copies so that nobody, including us, can quietly rewrite your history. That is the security property you are getting. Its price is that the KEL cannot be deleted, edited or holed once it has propagated. On its own it names a pseudonym, not a person: it contains no name, email or address.

4.2 Credentials are append-only

Work recorded, goods claimed, skills held, verification granted, relationships declared and votes cast are written as signed credentials, addressed by the hash of their contents and anchored in the issuing scope's KEL. Revoking a credential marks it invalid; it does not remove it. The fact that a credential was issued, and when, is permanent by design — that anchor is what makes the whole record auditable.

Deletion is not implemented today. The reference implementation has no automated path to erase a member record, a credential body or a stored key event. Erasure requests are handled by the operator manually, to the extent they can be honoured at all; §5 of the privacy notice sets out exactly what can and cannot be erased and what we are building to close the gap. Do not put anything into this system on the assumption that you can take it out again.

4.3 What you should not put in

Fields such as your display name, email, location and the free text you attach to needs, offers and proposals are shared with other participants and, through federation, with other gateways. Unless you intend them to be durably visible to your federation, do not enter:

  • your legal name, home address, or precise home location;

The profile and sharing features let you state things such as conditions, diet, position and home. They are optional, and each is described in the privacy notice. Enter them only if you accept that the people your settings let see them will.

5. The plan is a computation, not a promise

localchimera computes an allocation by convex optimization over the federation's recorded needs, capacities and limits. What comes out is a proposal — a plan the federation may adopt, amend or reject through its governance. Specifically:

5.1 The plan

  • an allocation shown to you is not an entitlement and creates no legal claim against the operator or any other member;
  • the planner's outputs may be wrong. It is optimization software under active development operating on data other members entered.

5.2 Labour credit

Labour credit is an internal accounting unit. It is not money, not a deposit, not e-money, not a security, not a wage and not a claim on anyone's assets. It is not redeemable for cash by us. It has no value outside the federation that recognises it, and a federation may revalue or discontinue it through governance.

5.3 Money, claims and payouts

Most of what the service does with value is bookkeeping: a record of an offer, a need or a claim. Recording an offer of money or means in the plan is a statement between members. Unless the product says otherwise, the operator does not take possession of it, does not act as an exchange or a bank, and takes no responsibility for what members agree among themselves.

Where this gateway has payouts enabled, one feature goes further. A claim is a ledger entry that the federation's plan lets you draw against. A payout turns part of a claim into a transfer of Monero (XMR) to an address you give us, sent from a wallet that the operator controls. You never hold a key to that wallet. Then:

  • a claim is an accounting entry. It is not a bank deposit, not e-money, not a security, and earns no interest;
  • payouts are made within the limits of the wallet's policy (spending caps, balance, network fees) and of the federation's rules. We may delay, reduce or refuse a payout where the law requires it, where we suspect fraud or error, where the destination or a party to it is subject to sanctions, or where the wallet cannot cover it;
  • a blockchain transfer cannot be reversed or recalled. You are responsible for the address you give; a payout to a wrong address is lost;
  • we neither change XMR into other currency nor hold it for you as a service. Its value can fall to nothing;
  • you are responsible for any tax, reporting or licensing that applies to what you receive or do with it. We give no tax, financial or investment advice.

A gateway without payouts enabled does none of this, and you will not see the feature. Where a claim path touches any other currency, the transaction is between you and the counterparty concerned.

5.4 No employment, no partnership

Recording hours of labour is accounting. Contributing labour to a commune does not by itself make you an employee, worker, contractor, agent, partner or joint venturer of the operator, of the software's authors, or of another member, and nothing in the service is an offer of work or of wages. Any such relationship needs its own written agreement. Your tax and social-security position is yours to settle, and different authorities may treat labour credit differently, so ask an adviser where it matters.

They are not legal, medical, nutritional, safety, engineering, tax or financial advice, and no adviser–client relationship arises from them.

Before you rely on any of it for something that matters — a permit, handling food for others, building, a tenancy, a medicine, a tax return, a payment — check with a qualified professional and the authority that governs where you are. 5.6 Health, nutrition and allergies

The service is a planning aid for a community's food and goods. It is not a medical device and is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, monitor or prevent any disease or condition, to set a diet or a dose, or to replace a clinician. The nutrient values it shows are published reference values, used to size and balance a commune's supply; they are averages, they may not fit you, and where you state a condition they are adjusted by tables that are not clinical guidance. For a condition such as kidney disease, malabsorption, diabetes, pregnancy or breastfeeding, or for an infant or a child, take the advice of your own clinician. Do not start, stop or change a medicine, a treatment or a therapeutic diet because of anything the service shows.

Allergens. Naming an allergen leaves it out of the menu the plan builds. That is planning and is not allergen safety: the service does not check labels, ingredients, preparation or cross-contact, and nobody who cooks, shares or hands over food through it is verified. If an allergy could harm you, read the label, ask the person who prepared the food, and carry what your clinician advises. Likewise, if you share food with others, you are responsible for telling them what is in it.

Stating health information is optional, and your explicit consent is what lets the plan use it (privacy notice, §3.2). In an emergency, stop and call your local emergency number.
