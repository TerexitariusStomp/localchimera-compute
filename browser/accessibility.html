Accessibility Statement

Version 1.0.0 · effective 2026-10-05 · for the gateway operated by [operator not configured — see docs/legal/operator.md]

This statement is written to be true rather than reassuring. playnet has not been audited against a formal accessibility standard. What follows is what is actually built, what is known to fall short, and how to tell us what we got wrong — not a conformance badge we have not earned.

1. Who this is for

This statement covers the playnet web application at this gateway's address: the dial, its faces, and the pages linked from it, including this one, the terms of use and the privacy notice. It does not cover a third-party page it links out to (a map tile provider, a notification service, a web search).

2. Standard, and status

The target is WCAG 2.1 level AA, the standard most accessibility law points to. We have not run a formal audit against it — no automated scan, no screen-reader pass, no assessment by someone who relies on assistive technology day to day. The honest status is not assessed, not "conformant" and not "partially conformant" in the formal sense those words carry in an accessibility statement, even though parts of the application are built with accessibility in mind, below.

3. What is actually built

  • Every interactive control in the app's shared component set — the chip buttons that make up most of the interface's choices (diet, body, location mode, housing, skills, and more) — is a real <button> element, reachable and operable by keyboard alone (Tab to reach it, Enter or Space to activate it), not a non-interactive element with a click handler bolted on.
  • Dialogs and sheets are marked role="dialog" with aria-modal="true" and a label, and controls whose meaning is not in their visible text (an icon-only button, a status mark) carry an aria-label or title — roughly 70 such attributes across the interface at last count, not a single pass added once and left to rot.
  • Every image the application itself renders carries alternative text.
  • Motion that is purely decorative respects a reduced-motion preference where the platform exposes one.
  • The onboarding walk and the settings pages are built as a sequence of labelled fields with a visible reason beside each ask, not a single dense form.

4. What is known to fall short

Said plainly, because a list of gaps is more useful than a blanket disclaimer:

  • Colour contrast has not been measured against the AA thresholds across every theme the interface offers (light, dark, and the look-and-feel options in settings). Some combinations may fall short.
  • The map, the recipe graph and other canvas- or SVG-heavy faces are visual by nature and have no complete non-visual equivalent; a screen-reader user can reach the data they show through other faces (the list and settings views) but not always through the visualisation itself.
  • No screen-reader testing has been done. ARIA attributes are present where we judged them necessary, but presence is not the same as a correct, tested reading order.
  • Live, frequently-updating regions (the plan recomposing, a live transcript, presence in a meeting) have not been checked for whether they announce sensibly to assistive technology rather than overwhelming it.
  • Voice and AI features (hold-to-speak, on-device transcription) are an accessibility aid for some and a barrier for others; every voice-driven action in the app also has a typed or tapped equivalent, by design, but we have not verified every one of them end to end.

5. Tell us what we got wrong

If something in this application is not usable for you, we want to know, specifically: what page or face, what you were trying to do, and what happened (or what assistive technology you were using, if you are able to say). Write to [operator contact not configured] — the same contact the terms of use (§7) and the privacy notice (§1) name. We answer within a reasonable time and will tell you honestly whether and when a fix is planned, the same way the rest of these notices do.

6. Alternatives

Everything the web application does is also reachable through the gateway's documented API (the door reference) and, for a member who prefers it, a terminal or voice interface where this gateway offers one. If the web interface is not usable for you right now, ask the contact above whether one of these covers what you need in the meantime.

7. Why this is published

Some places' law requires an accessibility statement for a service like this one; others do not, or the question has not been settled for this operator (see docs/legal/operator.md §10). We publish it regardless, because the alternative — saying nothing — tells a member nothing about whether the application was built with them in mind, and the honest answer is partly yes, partly not yet.
