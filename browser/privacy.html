Privacy Notice

Version 1.1.0 · effective 2026-10-05 · for the gateway operated by [operator not configured — see docs/legal/README.md]

This notice is written to be true rather than reassuring. playnet's record is cryptographically append-only: that is what makes it auditable, and it is also why several things you may expect from a privacy notice — a delete button, a guarantee that a record has gone everywhere it went — do not exist here. §5 states plainly what we can and cannot do, and what is being built to narrow the gap.
No real-world exchange of food, shelter, a ride, labour or money has been organized through this deployment yet — it is open today for interface testing. That does not change what this notice describes below: an account created now is real, and the data in it is held and processed as this notice says from the moment you create it. While that is true, please do not use your legal name or other real contact details — the identity screen asks for a name because some surfaces use one, not because it needs to be yours; a nickname is fine.

1. Who is responsible for your data

playnet is federated: anyone can run a gateway, and the software's authors run none of them. The controller of your personal data is the operator of the gateway you use — here, [operator not configured]. The authors of the software are not a controller and hold none of your data.

Contact for anything in this notice, including any request under §5: [operator contact not configured]. Postal address, where the operator has one: [not configured]. Representative or data-protection officer, where one is designated: [not configured].

Other gateways are separate controllers. When credentials are shared across the federation, the receiving gateway determines its own purposes for them and answers for them itself. Where two gateways jointly decide how shared credentials are used, they are likely joint controllers under Article 26 GDPR, and each should tell you the essence of that arrangement. Ask us and we will tell you which gateways we federate with.

2.2 Credentials — the substantive record

Most of what the system knows about you is held as signed credentials, stored by the hash of their contents and anchored in the issuing scope's key event log:

  • work events — hours of labour, per person, per process. This is working-time data;
  • claim events — what you drew from the commons, how much, and when. Taken together this is a consumption profile;
  • skills — competences you hold, with a validity window;
  • personhood — an attestation that your AID is a distinct real person, and who sponsored you. Where an identity check was done, we store a hash of the evidence, never the evidence itself;
  • relationships — declared trust, colleagues, guardians and delegates: an explicit, signed social graph;
  • stances — how you voted on a proposal, and any delegation of your voice (§3);
  • contact points — where you choose to hold a channel as your own credential rather than leaving it in our member record.

2.3 On your own device

  • Your private keys are generated in your browser as non-extractable keys and kept in the browser's own key store. The private half never becomes readable bytes, so it cannot be copied out of the page — not by us, and not by an attacker who gets script into it.
  • Your passcode is never stored anywhere and never sent to us. It is the key.
  • A local sign-in hint holds your AID and, if you set them, your name and email in plain text in this browser's storage. It sits on your device only. Clearing site data removes it — and, since you will then need your AID and passcode to sign in again, do not clear it before you have saved them.
  • Your consent to these documents (the version, the time and your AID) is recorded in your own browser. We do not yet hold a copy; see §6.

2.4 Location, in particular

You choose what is shared. The location panel offers live location, "here, now" and "not shared". Choose not shared to stop. A shared fix is filed at the nearest known place within a short distance of you, or else as your own place on a grid of about one kilometre, named for the nearest place we know. One place is kept for you and replaced as you move, so the service keeps where you are and no trail. Your home, if you set it, is kept the same way. Your device may ask your permission before it gives the browser its location; refusing is always possible, and the service works with a place you pick by hand. Who sees your position depends on the scope you share it into, and it reaches other gateways only as far as the federation shares that scope.

3. Special-category data

3.1 Political opinions

How you vote is recorded against your identity, in signed form. Under Article 9 GDPR that is special-category data and needs a specific condition to process at all. The condition relied on is Article 9(2)(d): processing by a not-for-profit body with a political or philosophical aim, in the course of its legitimate activities, concerning its own members, with no disclosure outside the body without your consent. Where the operator is not a body of that kind, the condition is instead your explicit consent — Article 9(2)(a) — asked once, separately from the terms, either when you create your identity or the first time you cast a vote.

What that means in practice, and its limits:

  • a stance credential carries a high-entropy blinding value, so the hash that identifies it can no longer be brute-forced back to how you voted — a real leak, now closed;
  • this makes the ballot secret against a bystander holding the identifier. It is not secret against the tallying scope, which must read the stance to count it;
  • ballot snapshots record voting weight and the resolved delegation chain per member. Keeping these inside the tallying scope rather than distributing them across the federation is current work, not a finished property — treat your vote as visible to your own commune.

You can withdraw that consent at any time — settings, "your data and the notices," the toggle for it — as easily as you gave it, per Article 7(3). Withdrawing does not erase a vote already recorded (§5.2 says what cannot be erased); it means you will be asked again before the next one. If you do not want a political opinion recorded against your identity at all, do not vote. Abstaining is the only complete protection we can honestly offer.

3.2 Health, and what else can reveal it

You may state conditions, allergies, diet or abilities in your profile so the planner can meet needs that depend on them. That is health data, and it is optional. The condition relied on is your explicit consent, Article 9(2)(a), given when you enter it, and you may withdraw it by removing the tag. Other things you enter — a diet, a belief, who you trust, where you are — can reveal sensitive characteristics too, such as religion, health or sexual life. The planner reads profile tags to match needs, and other members of your commune may see them as the space allows. Enter only what you accept being seen by those people, and by anyone a member shares it with. The service is a planning aid and not a medical device; the notices beside these fields in the app, and §5.6 of the terms, say what it cannot do for your health.

4. Who else receives it

4.1 Witnesses, and the public discovery endpoint

Every key event is broadcast to witnesses — independent machines run by other people, in jurisdictions we do not choose or know — which keep copies so that no single party, including us, can rewrite your key history. Your key event log is also served at this gateway's discovery endpoints, without authentication: anyone who knows your AID can fetch it. It contains your public keys, rotation history and timestamps; it does not contain your name or email.

Once a key event has been broadcast, it cannot be recalled. Verification of your signatures requires an unbroken sequence of key events, so events cannot be removed even locally without destroying the ability to check anything you signed afterwards. The timing and existence of your identity's events are therefore permanent. Requiring authentication on the discovery endpoint is planned and is anticipated by the KERI specification; it is not in place today.

4.2 Other gateways in the federation

Credentials are shared with federated gateways so that work, skills and personhood recognised in one commune are recognised in another. Those gateways are run by other people under their own notices and their own law.

5. Your rights — and exactly where they stop

You have the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection, and the right to withdraw consent. Exercise any of them by writing to [operator contact not configured]. We answer within one month; where a request is complex or numerous we may extend that by two further months and tell you why. We do not charge for requests unless they are manifestly unfounded or excessive.

5.1 What we can do

  • Access and portability. We can give you the member record, notification bindings and credentials we hold for your AID, in a structured machine-readable form.
  • Rectification. You can change your display name and email yourself in settings; a credential cannot be edited, so correcting one means revoking it and issuing a corrected replacement.
  • Erasure of the link between your AID and you. This is the meaningful erasure available here. Removing the member record and the AID-to-name index leaves behind a pseudonym that nobody can resolve to a person. Combined with dropping your notification bindings, presence entry and push subscription, it removes essentially all the directly identifying data we hold.
  • Objection and restriction for our own processing on this gateway.

5.2 What we cannot do

These are architectural limits, not policy choices, and we will not pretend otherwise.
  • Key events cannot be erased. They are held by independent witnesses and, locally, holes in the sequence would break verification of everything you ever signed.
  • The fact and timing of every credential issuance is permanent, because it is anchored in a key event log. Removing a credential's contents can never remove the record that something was issued at that moment.
  • Copies at other gateways are beyond our control. We will pass on your erasure request to gateways we know received your data, but we cannot compel them.
  • A payout cannot be recalled. A transaction on the Monero blockchain is permanent.
  • Automated deletion is not implemented today. Every store in the reference implementation is append-only; revoking a credential flips its status and leaves the body on disk. Erasure requests are carried out manually by the operator, within these limits.

5.3 What is being built

Stated so you can judge the gap rather than take a promise on trust:

  • a forget operation that drops the member record, index, notification bindings, presence and push subscription in one step, and best-effort notifies the gateways that received your data;
  • encryption of credential bodies at rest under a per-person key, so that destroying that key makes the contents unrecoverable while the signatures, links and anchors stay verifiable — the erasure route the KERI specification itself sanctions ("only copies of the digest or signature of the data need to be kept");
  • blinding values on every credential carrying personal data, so a destroyed body cannot be reconstructed by guessing at its identifier;
  • moving contact points into credentials you hold and disclose revocably, rather than records we keep — after which withdrawing your email is something you do, not something you ask us for. The credential layer for this exists; the interface for it does not yet.

The rights above come from the GDPR. The operator applies them to every member, wherever they live. Where the law of your country gives you more, you have it:

  • United Kingdom and Switzerland — the UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018, and the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection, give rights equivalent to those above. Complaints go to the Information Commissioner's Office or the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner.
  • California and other US states — you may ask to know, correct and delete the personal information held about you, and to opt out of its sale or sharing. We do not sell personal information and do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, so there is nothing to opt out of; we do not treat you differently for asking. Complaints may go to your state attorney general or privacy regulator.
  • Brazil, Canada, Australia and elsewhere — the LGPD, PIPEDA and provincial laws such as Quebec's, the Privacy Act 1988 and their counterparts give rights of access, correction and deletion, and a route to the national regulator (the ANPD, the Office of the Privacy Commissioner, the OAIC). 7. Retention

Stated honestly: no retention period is enforced today for the member records described in §2.1 and the credentials in §2.2. Credential validity windows — a personhood credential's expiry, a skill's valid-until — govern whether a credential still counts, not whether it is still stored; a lapsed credential remains readable on disk. This is a known defect. Server logs are rotated hourly with their identifying fields already redacted (§9); what is not yet built is a retention sweep for the member and credential stores themselves, and member data kept only while you participate. Records the law requires us to keep, such as those of a payout, are kept for as long as it requires. Key events and credential anchors are kept for good, for the reasons in §4.1.

8. Automated decisions

The planner computes allocations automatically — by convex optimization over recorded needs, capacities, limits and contributed labour — and those allocations affect what you can claim from the commons. We consider this not to be a decision "based solely on automated processing" within Article 22 GDPR, because the output is a proposal that the federation adopts, amends or rejects through its governance, in which you have a voice and a vote. Either way, you can ask a person to look at any allocation that affects you, express your point of view and contest it — through the governance process, or by writing to the contact in §1. AI features draft or read for you and decide nothing about you.

9. Security

  • Private keys are generated non-extractable and never leave your device; the gateway has no key custody by design.
  • Every event and credential is signed, so tampering is detectable rather than merely forbidden.
  • Traffic is encrypted in transit; the gateway is also reachable as an onion service.
  • Server logs are rotated hourly, compressed, and held only on volatile storage; identifying fields (an AID, an email address) are reduced to a short, non-identifying tag before a line is written, never the value itself.
  • Known gaps, being fixed: the discovery endpoint is unauthenticated; credential bodies are stored unencrypted.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach is likely to put your rights at risk we will notify the supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, and, where the risk is high, you without undue delay.

10. International transfers

Witnesses and federated gateways are run by independent people in places we do not choose, and mesh transport routes wherever the mesh routes. Publishing to them is inherent to how the system resists tampering. No adequacy decision, standard contractual clauses or other safeguard covers these flows, and the law of those places may give you less protection than yours does. When you create an identity you ask us, knowing that, to publish your key events to witnesses and to share your credentials with federated gateways, and that is the explicit consent on which we rely for these transfers (Article 49(1)(a) GDPR); it is a derogation for a reason, so we limit what is sent to what the protocol needs. If that is unacceptable to you, this system is not suitable for you — we would rather say so than bury it.

Where the operator uses a provider outside your country or the EEA for hosting, AI, notifications or maps, it relies on an adequacy decision, standard contractual clauses or comparable safeguards where they are available, and otherwise on your consent as above. Ask us for the details.

12. Cookies and local storage

No advertising, analytics or third-party tracking cookies are used, and the service does not track you across other sites, so it treats a "Do Not Track" or Global Privacy Control signal as honoured. The page keeps data in your browser's own storage — your sign-in hint, your keys, your interface preferences, and a cache of the app so it works offline — all of which stays on your device. This is what the service you asked for needs, which is why it does not ask for a cookie consent. You can clear it in your browser's settings.
