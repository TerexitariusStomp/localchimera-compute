Foundational premise

Science is humanity's engagement with nature becoming conscious of itself

The nature that science studies is never a bare "given" standing apart from human history — it is nature as already shaped by labor, industry, and social practice. Correspondingly, the scientist is not a disembodied observer, but a sensuous, historically formed being whose capacities of perception and reasoning are themselves products of cumulative human development.

Inquiry that forgets either fact produces knowledge that is abstract and alienated — technically functional, perhaps, but false to its own foundations. The aim of science is the development and confirmation of human powers: to know the world is, at the same time, to expand what human beings are capable of being and doing.

Every finding is therefore recorded twice — once along the technical axis, once along the human axis — and judged complete only where the two agree.

Technical axis

Internal coherence, measurement, control, replication, predictive power. Rigor is not relaxed; it is joined to reflexivity, never substituted by it.

Human axis

The history sedimented in the object, the standpoint of the investigator, and the bearing of the work on human capacity and freedom.

Recipes & Repetitions

A single inquiry is not yet science — knowledge is confirmed in practice, through repetition. Each experiment lives here as a recipe: a repeatable design exposed to chosen stressors. Every run of the recipe is a repetition that records the stress actually experienced and the fraction of available value captured (the minimax-regret ground: how close did this run come to the best achievable in its circumstances?). The grid below never takes a recipe's word for its own character — it classifies each one as fragile, robust, or antifragile with respect to the filtered stressor, computed from the observed repetitions alone.

Closing principle

Where science and life diverge, the fault lies in the science

At every stage the same test applies: does this act of knowing confirm and expand human powers, or does it require treating human life as external to truth? To found the two on separate grounds is, from the outset, a lie.
