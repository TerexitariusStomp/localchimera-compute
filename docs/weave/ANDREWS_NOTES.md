# A collection of observations and suggestions from Andrew
* Variable names like s0 follow classic Lisp conventions from the 1990s but are unnecessarily (and maybe intentionally) obscure. Such concise variable names are characteristic of early software development where verbosity created real costs. Now the biggest cost is cryptic language that interferes with rapid human interpretation. I'd recommend doing a global find-replace to make variable names as clear and explicit as possible. PURISM IS IDEOLOGY.
* The choice of Scheme as the language has a theoretical beauty and purity. It brings two massive risks: The difficulty of finding people who can read and contribute (although LLMs can help a lot), lots of unknowns related to scaling.
* The choice to unleash infinite emergence and mutability (programs changing themselves) is a terrifying design decision. It may make debugging and understanding production programs extremely difficult. At the same time, it brings some unique potential, and maps to the amount of complexity in the real world. YOLO!?
